Face-to-Face Meetings, Worker Mobility and Referrals

with Eleonora Patacchini

CEPR Discussion Paper

Abstract. We study how different communication technologies shape the labor-market consequences of social networks. Read more Show less

We develop a framework in which communication methods vary in how effectively existing social ties support workplace mobility. Using cellphone geolocation and sociodemographic data covering 3.3 million workers in Beijing, we measure the relative importance of face-to-face contact and examine job transitions within friendship networks. A greater face-to-face communication share predicts a higher likelihood of following a friend to a workplace, but only after that friend has joined the destination. The results show that the labor-market value of a social connection depends not just on who workers know, but also on how they communicate.

Beyond the Target: U.S. Sanctions, Inventor Mobility, and the Unintended Knowledge Diffusion

with Di Lu, Yanzhe Wang and Mingzhi Xu

SSRN

Abstract. Targeted technology controls aim to constrain sanctioned firms, but may diffuse inventive capacity beyond them. Read more Show less

We examine this unintended effect using additions of Chinese firms to the U.S. Entity List and a comprehensive inventor–firm panel drawn from Chinese patents between 2014 and 2022. Listing increases inventor departures by 8.1 percentage points without lowering movers’ patent output, especially at firms that depend heavily on U.S. inputs or have narrower knowledge bases. Recipient firms absorb knowledge from movers’ former employers, redirect their innovation, increase incumbent patenting, and intensify competition in innovation markets. These spillovers offset more than one quarter of the sanctioned firms’ innovation losses.

Social Media Disputes, Toxicity and Spillovers

with Juanjuan Meng, Yue Wang and Liufang Xu

SSRN

Abstract. Online disputes are pervasive on social media, yet their effects on user welfare remain largely unexplored. Read more Show less

Using 8.9 million Weibo posts from 30,516 users, we study fan-group disputes with a stacked difference-in-differences design based on plausibly exogenous dispute shocks. Disputes increase toxic expression and late-night posting, with particularly strong effects among younger users, women, and opinion leaders. The evidence is consistent with identity reinforcement: users direct more toxicity toward out-groups while increasing consumption-related support for the focal celebrity. The effects also spill across topics and persist over time, extending beyond the dispute that triggered them.
